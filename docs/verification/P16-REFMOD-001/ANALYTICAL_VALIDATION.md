# P16-REFMOD-001 — analytical oracles

```text
SUBJECT:  where every expected number comes from, and why none of it comes
          from the code under test
SOURCE:   tests/reference/Analytic.hpp  -- which opens with "Nothing here uses
          BetterCAD or the kernel"
```

## The rule, and how it is enforced rather than promised

The brief's critical rule is that expected values must not be generated by the
implementation being tested. In this repository that is structural rather than a
matter of discipline: `tests/reference/Analytic.hpp` includes no BetterCAD
header and no OCCT header, so it *cannot* call a production volume function.
P16 adds a millimetre-based section to it and restates none of the three SI
closed forms it already carried.

## The chain is four-way

```text
the model's DIMENSIONS, read from its own parameters
    |
    v  a closed form that knows nothing about BetterCAD
INDEPENDENT ANALYTIC VOLUME
    |
    +--> compared with the catalog's DECLARED volume      a typo in either
    |
    +--> compared with the KERNEL's CAD volume            OCCT, as a second
    |                                                     implementation
    +--> compared with the TETRAHEDRAL volume             the real gate
```

Each link catches something the others cannot. A suite that compared the mesh
against `mesh.cadVolume()` would be asking OCCT to mark its own work; one that
took the expected volume from the catalog alone could not tell a builder that
builds the wrong block from a catalog that declares the wrong volume.

The dimensions are read with `mm(document, "block_a")` — a parameter lookup.
Nothing in `analyticVolumeOf` reads a volume.

## pi

```text
analytic::pi = std::numbers::pi
```

Exactly what the brief asks for. The declared volumes in the catalog use
`kPi = 3.14159265358979323846`, the same value to the last bit of a double, and
`MeshReferenceSuite_DeclaredVolumesAgreeWithTheIndependentClosedForms` requires
the declaration and the derivation to agree to 1e-15 relative — so the two
spellings cannot drift.

## The curved models get a derived bound, not a tolerance

A tetrahedral mesh of a curved body does not have the body's volume and never
will: the boundary is a polyhedron whose faces are chords of the true surface.
So "the mesh volume equals the analytic volume" is the wrong expectation, and a
percentage chosen because it passed would be exactly what the brief forbids.

What *is* available is a two-sided bound that follows from the declared surface
deflection alone. A chord of a circle of radius `r` whose deepest deviation from
the arc is at most `d` has its closest approach to the centre at `r - d`, so an
inscribed chord polygon

```text
contains  the disc of radius r - d
lies in   the disc of radius r
```

and so has area between `pi(r - d)^2` and `pi r^2`. Nothing is fitted, no
segment count has to be known, and **the direction of the error follows from
which side of the material the curved surface is on** — which is why a hole's
bound runs the opposite way from a boss's, and a check written for one would
fail the other.

```text
RM-MESH-02   pi (r - d)^2 h  <=  V  <=  pi r^2 h
RM-MESH-03   LWt - pi r^2 t  <=  V  <=  LWt - pi (r - d)^2 t
RM-MESH-04   pi((Ro - d)^2 - Ri^2) h  <=  V  <=  pi(Ro^2 - (Ri - d)^2) h
```

With `d = 0.25 mm`, the declared deflection of all three curved models:

```text
MODEL        BOUND, mm^3                       MEASURED V, mm^3    WIDTH     WHERE
RM-MESH-02   [115465.31099187586,              117212.51992517807  2.010e-2  inside,
              117809.72450961724]                                            below
RM-MESH-03   [63517.699835307561,              63560.69856538716   4.405e-3  inside,
              63798.086979640444]                                            ABOVE
RM-MESH-04   [79318.342269212546,              81017.293772282923  4.167e-2  inside
              82693.590876413087]
```

RM-MESH-03's lower bound **is** the analytic volume, which makes the direction
of the error a prediction rather than an allowance: a mesh below it would mean
the hole had been cut too big, and a mesh that *filled* the hole would read
72000 — which exceeds the permitted upper bound by **29.32 times the bound's own
width**. The suite measures that ratio and gates it at 20x, so the tolerance
demonstrably cannot absorb the defect it exists to catch.

## Convergence, and a correction

The brief asks that geometric approximation be shown to behave consistently, and
forbids declaring convergence from element count. RM-MESH-02's study varies the
**surface deflection**, which is what controls a curved body's approximation:

```text
LEVEL    DEFLECTION  FACETS  NODES  TET4  Vmesh mm^3           REL ERROR
coarse   0.5 mm        140    114    368  117212.51992517788   5.069e-03
medium   0.1 mm        196    200    732  117499.90646653521   2.630e-03
fine     0.02 mm       444    442   1695  117747.93919810280   5.245e-04
```

The study's global target is **80 mm**, coarser than the body, and not the
model's declared 12 mm. A curved body's volume error is the chord error of its
boundary, so the volume target contributes nothing to what is being measured —
and at 12 mm the finest level cost 3772 tetrahedra and **245 seconds per run** in
a debug build, which the suite would then have paid in three presets and five
repeats for ever. Letting the boundary govern gives the same 140 / 196 / 444
facets, the same volumes to fifteen significant figures and the same relative
errors, in **6 seconds**.

> **A defect in this test, found and fixed before the freeze.** The first
> version used 0.5, 0.25 and 0.1 mm — and 0.5 and 0.25 produced the **same 140
> boundary facets**, because OCCT's 20-degree angular limit was binding at both.
> The two volumes then differed only in their last two digits
> (117212.51992517796 against 117212.51992517807), so the assertion "the error
> fell" passed on floating-point noise. The suite now asserts that each level
> has **strictly more boundary facets** than the last, before comparing any
> errors, and the levels were changed to ones that genuinely differ. The margin
> is now a factor of ten rather than one unit in the last place.

## RM-MESH-01's sizing levels: the honest result

```text
LEVEL    TARGET  NODES  TET4  MEDIAN EDGE  VOLUME ERROR
coarse    30 mm      8     6     120 mm      1.980e-16
medium    20 mm      8     6     120 mm      1.980e-16
fine      10 mm      8     6     120 mm      1.980e-16
```

**They are the same mesh.** OCCT triangulates a planar face with two triangles
whatever the deflection, so a block's boundary is irreducibly its own edge
lengths and a global target has nowhere to act. P16-SIZE-001 measured and pinned
this, and it is the approved pipeline working as designed: a validated surface
goes in and the backend only fills it.

So this model has no convergence to declare, and the suite records that with the
reason rather than omitting the levels — or, worse, claiming convergence because
an element count moved. The volume is exact at every level, which is the real
statement a planar model makes about sizing.

## Scale

```text
s = 2.5 on RM-MESH-01, with the global target scaled equivalently

analytic 294000 -> 4593750 mm^3 = 294000 x s^3            exact
mesh     294000.00000000006 -> 4593749.9999999991
CAD      matched to 1e-12 relative
worst radius ratio   0.43725 -> 0.43725                   invariant to 1e-6
min dihedral         14.6211 -> 14.6211 deg               invariant to 1e-6
```

Dimensionless metrics are scale-free, which a unit-conversion defect anywhere in
the sizing chain would break while leaving the volume intact.
